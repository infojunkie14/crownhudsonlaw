Since 2019, every foreign-domiciled applicant or registrant before the USPTO must be represented by a U.S.-licensed attorney.

Crown Hudson Law is that counsel. We clear, file, and prosecute U.S. trademarks for brand owners abroad, and serve as U.S. counsel of record for the clients of the foreign IP firms that represent them.

Flat fees, quoted in writing before any work begins.

The Practice
01

U.S. registration for
foreign applicants

We handle clearance, classification and the identification of goods and services, filing, and prosecution through examination. Applicants domiciled outside the United States must be represented before the USPTO by a U.S.-licensed attorney (37 C.F.R. § 2.11); the firm represents U.S.-domiciled applicants as well.

U.S. registration in detail →
02

USPTO Office
Action responses

Many applications draw an Office Action. We answer Office Actions of every kind, § 2(d) likelihood-of-confusion and § 2(e)(1) descriptiveness refusals among them, and say plainly whether a refusal is worth arguing, worth amending around, or worth letting go.

Office Action responses in detail →
03

Madrid § 66(a)
provisional refusals

When a request for extension of protection to the United States draws a provisional refusal, the holder must respond through U.S. counsel of record, and the response period cannot be extended. We review the refusal, advise on the strongest course, and file the response.
