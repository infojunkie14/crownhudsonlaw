The service and the parties

Brand owners may apply directly. U.S. and foreign law firms may request client-authorized coordination. Referring branding, e-commerce or incorporation advisers should ask the trademark owner to complete the form and name the referral source below. The law-firm fields are for authorized legal counsel.

Provide the notices and use or title documents you have. The attorney verifies eligibility, deadlines and authority before agreeing the scope.

1. The mark

What kind of mark is it?

The USPTO requires a written description of any mark that is not plain words (37 CFR 2.37). Name a color here only if you are claiming color below.

Is color part of what you want to protect?

Claiming color narrows the registration to those colors. Most applicants do not claim color unless the color itself is what customers recognize (TMEP 807.07).

Does the mark include a word from a language other than English, or letters that are not Latin?
Is any part of the mark the name, nickname, signature, or likeness of a real person?
Is that person living, and have they agreed in writing to the mark?
Does the mark name or suggest a place (a city, region, or country)?
Do the goods or services actually come from there?

Answer this even if the mark has nothing to do with a place. If the mark contains a place name, this is the answer that decides how it is handled (15 U.S.C. 1052(e)(2) and (e)(3)).

Does the mark include a flag, coat of arms, or government insignia?
Is the mark, or a word in it, also somebody's surname?

Whether anyone connected with the business carries the name is one of the things the USPTO weighs (TMEP 1211.02(b)(iv)), and no database can tell us. You can.

Does any word in the mark plainly describe what you sell, where it is made, or a quality of it?

2. What you sell under it

Type a product or service, one at a time. The list is the USPTO's own wording, grouped by class. Tick every entry you sell today or expect to sell within about three years under this mark. The list can be narrowed after filing but never broadened (37 CFR 2.71(a)), so something left out now needs a new application with a later date. Fees are set per class; the count updates as you tick.

Your chosen entries

Do you make these products (or have them made for your own brand), or do you resell products made by other brands?
Do you also do any of these for other businesses, under this same mark? Tick all that apply.

Answer this even if it feels obvious. Selling your own products is the goods class. Doing any of the first three for somebody else is a separate service in Class 35 with its own fee and its own protection, and it is the one thing the class list cannot work out from a description of your products.

For software or an app: how do customers get it?
Who buys it, and where?

3. Who will own the registration

The owner is whoever controls how the mark is used, usually the operating company rather than its founder.
Is the mark used in the U.S. by anyone other than the applicant (a distributor, a licensee, a related company, a franchisee)?

Use by a company you control, or that uses the mark under your license and quality control, can count as your use (15 U.S.C. 1055; TMEP 1201.03). Use by an unrelated distributor in its own name can raise an ownership question.

We confirm the entity's name and standing against the public registry of that state or country before anything is filed.

Domicile address

For a company, the place where its senior officers actually direct and control the business, usually the head office (37 CFR 2.2(p)); for an individual, where you actually live (2.2(o)). A street address. The USPTO does not accept a P.O. box, a private or commercial mailbox (PMB, CMRA), a military post office (APO, FPO, DPO), a mail-forwarding service, a registered-agent address, a virtual office or a "care of" address, and can ask for proof (37 CFR 2.189). This is the one answer the rules attach a penalty to: false or fictitious domicile information is treated as a paper filed for an improper purpose (37 CFR 2.11(e)). A wrong guess is worse than "unknown"; if you are not sure, say what you know in the note below.

That reads like a box or forwarding address. Give the street where the business is actually run, and explain below if there is no such address.

The USPTO's practice has been to keep the domicile address out of public view when a separate mailing address is also given. Give both so a home address can be kept private where the rules allow.

The USPTO requires an email address for the applicant itself, separate from its attorney's (37 CFR 2.32(a)(2)), and sends reminders about future maintenance filings to it. Choose one that will still be read years from now.

4. Registrations elsewhere

Does the applicant own a registration or pending application for this mark in another country?

5. History

Has the applicant, or anyone connected to it, filed a U.S. application for this mark or a similar one before?
Has anyone ever objected to your use of the mark, or have you objected to someone else's?
Did you ever receive a USPTO letter saying you must appoint a U.S.-licensed attorney, or one saying an application was abandoned, cancelled or terminated?

Some of these situations have deadlines that are still running (37 CFR 2.62 and 2.66; TMEP 711 and 1714). We confirm the current period for your file at engagement.

Has the applicant signed any agreement with another party about this mark (a coexistence or consent agreement, a license, an assignment or purchase, a settlement)?

Any of these can change who may file, what can be claimed and what the USPTO needs to see. If the mark was bought from someone else, we need the assignment document.

6. Conflicts

Before accepting any matter, the attorney checks the names below against the firm's records of current and former clients and the marks the firm has worked on. The check relies on what you disclose here. If a conflict appears, we tell you promptly by email, without details of the other matter, and we do not start work. What you tell us is kept confidential whether or not we take the matter (22 NYCRR 1200, Rule 1.18). Separate names with semicolons; write "none" where that is the answer.

7. You

New York's rules on attorney advertising and solicitation require the firm to keep a record of how it reached each client (22 NYCRR 1200, Rules 7.1 and 7.3), and this answer is that record.

8. Before you send

Before anything is filed: the USPTO's own fee comes from your payment method, not ours.

Why it works this way. Money a lawyer holds for a client is client money and has to sit in a trust account. The firm does not hold your filing fees at all, so that question never arises and your money is never mixed with the firm's. The USPTO's system is called Financial Manager, it is free, and you stay in control: you own the payment method, you see every charge against it, and you can remove the firm's permission at any time without asking us. We send step-by-step instructions when your file is ready.
