Questions, Answered Plainly

Frequently asked questions.

General information about U.S. trademark practice. For advice on your own matter, write to the firm.

Does a foreign business need a U.S. attorney to file a USPTO trademark application?

Yes. Since August 2019, U.S. law (37 C.F.R. § 2.11) has required every trademark applicant, registrant, or party domiciled outside the United States to be represented before the USPTO by an attorney licensed in the United States. Non-lawyer filing services cannot lawfully represent applicants, whatever their websites suggest.

What happens if my U.S. application receives an Office Action?

An Office Action is a letter from the USPTO examining attorney refusing registration or requiring changes. It carries a strict response deadline, and missing it abandons the application. Most refusals are answerable: some by argument, some by amendment. We review the letter and tell you plainly which path fits, and whether the fight is worth having, before quoting a flat fee for the response. More on Office Action responses.

What is a Madrid Protocol §66(a) provisional refusal?

When an international registration designates the United States under the Madrid Protocol, the USPTO examines it like any U.S. application. If the examiner finds a problem, a provisional refusal issues and reaches you through WIPO. Responding requires a U.S.-licensed attorney as counsel of record. It looks alarming; it is an ordinary, answerable step. More on Madrid refusals.

Do you meet clients in person or by phone?

The practice is fully online by design. Intake, advice, and reporting happen in writing, by email, which gives clients in any time zone a complete documented record of every step, with no scheduling across midnight.

How are your fees structured?

Every engagement is a flat fee stated in a written engagement before any work begins. USPTO government fees are separate and passed through at cost. The firm does not bill hourly.

Can you take over an application filed by someone else?

Yes. We can appear as counsel of record on applications originally filed by another attorney, by a filing service, or by you directly, and respond to whatever is pending.

Can you guarantee my trademark will register?

No, and no honest attorney can. Registration is decided by the USPTO after examination. What we can promise is a candid risk assessment before filing, careful work, and plain advice at every decision point, including the advice not to spend money on a weak position.
