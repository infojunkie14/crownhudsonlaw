Frequently Asked Questions

Questions brand owners abroad ask about U.S. trademarks.

General information about U.S. trademark practice. For advice on your own matter, write to the firm.

Does a foreign applicant need a U.S. attorney to file a USPTO trademark application?

Yes. Since August 2019, USPTO rules (37 C.F.R. § 2.11) have required every trademark applicant, registrant, or party to a proceeding domiciled outside the United States to be represented before the USPTO by an attorney licensed in the United States. Non-lawyer filing services cannot lawfully represent applicants.

What happens if my U.S. application receives an Office Action?

An Office Action is a letter from the USPTO examining attorney refusing registration or requiring changes. It carries a strict response deadline, and missing it abandons the application. Most refusals are answerable: some by argument, some by amendment. We review the letter, tell you which path fits and whether the fight is worth having, then confirm the flat fee for the response in writing. More on Office Action responses.

What is a Madrid Protocol Section 66(a) provisional refusal?

When an international registration designates the United States under the Madrid Protocol, the USPTO examines it like any U.S. application. If the examining attorney finds a problem, a provisional refusal issues and reaches you through WIPO. Responding requires a U.S.-licensed attorney as counsel of record. It is an ordinary step in U.S. examination, and the response period cannot be extended. More on Madrid refusals.

How does the firm work with clients in other time zones?

Crown Hudson Law is a fully online practice. Intake, advice, and reporting happen in writing, which gives a client in any time zone a complete record of every step, to read at whatever hour suits them.

How are your fees structured?

Every matter is handled for a flat fee, stated in the engagement letter before work begins. USPTO filing fees are separate and passed through at cost. We do not bill hourly.

Can you take over an application that someone else filed?

Yes. We can appear as counsel of record on applications originally filed by another attorney, by a filing service, or by you directly, and respond to whatever examination issue is pending.

Will my trademark register?

No attorney can tell you that in advance. Registration is decided by the USPTO after examination. What we can offer is a candid risk assessment before filing, a thorough response to whatever the examining attorney raises, and plain advice at every decision point, including the advice not to spend money on a weak position.
