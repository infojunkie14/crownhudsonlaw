USPTO Office Action responses.

A refusal is not the end of an application. It is a letter that deserves a careful answer.

What an Office Action is

Many U.S. trademark applications receive at least one Office Action: a letter from the examining attorney refusing registration or requiring changes before the application can proceed. The response deadline is strict and short, and an application that misses it is abandoned. The letter is dense with citations and written for U.S. practitioners. Reading it correctly, and knowing which arguments move an examining attorney, is most of the job.

What we answer

Office Actions of every kind, including:

  • § 2(d) likelihood of confusion: the examining attorney believes your mark is too close to an existing registration, or to an earlier-filed application that could block it once registered.
  • § 2(e) refusals: marks refused as merely descriptive, deceptively misdescriptive, primarily geographically descriptive, or primarily merely a surname.
  • Specimen refusals: the proof of use does not show the mark in use in commerce for the goods or services listed, or does not show it functioning as a trademark.
  • Identification and classification requirements: goods and services that are indefinite, misclassified, or too broad.
  • Disclaimer requirements, ornamentation refusals, and procedural requirements: the formalities that catch unrepresented applicants, including the U.S.-counsel requirement itself.
  • Provisional refusals on Madrid Protocol extensions: Section 66(a) filings raise the same grounds, on their own timetable and with narrower room to amend.

The read before the fee

Before you pay for a response, you get our read: whether the refusal is worth arguing, better handled by amendment, or sometimes not worth the money at all. A response we do not believe in is a response we will tell you not to buy.

Applications we did not file

Matters that began elsewhere are welcome. If your application was filed by another attorney, by a filing service, or on your own, we can appear as counsel and respond.

The fee

Flat and per response, published on the pricing page and confirmed in writing after we read the Office Action, before any work begins.
