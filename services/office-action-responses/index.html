USPTO Office Action responses.

A refusal is not the end of an application. It is a letter that deserves a careful, honest answer.

What an Office Action is

Most U.S. trademark applications receive at least one Office Action: a letter from the examining attorney refusing registration or requiring changes before the application can proceed. The response deadline is strict and short, and an application that misses it goes abandoned. The letter is dense, citation-heavy, and written for lawyers. Reading it correctly, and knowing which arguments actually move an examiner, is the whole job.

What we answer

Office Actions of every kind, including:

  • § 2(d) likelihood of confusion: the examiner believes your mark is too close to an existing registration or earlier application.
  • § 2(e) refusals: marks refused as merely descriptive, deceptively misdescriptive, geographically descriptive, or primarily a surname.
  • Specimen refusals: the proof of use does not show the mark functioning as a trademark for the goods or services.
  • Identification and classification requirements: goods and services that are indefinite, misclassified, or too broad.
  • Disclaimer requirements, ornamentation refusals, and the procedural requirements that trip up unrepresented applicants, including the U.S.-counsel requirement itself.

The honest-triage promise

Before you pay for a response, you get our plain read: whether the refusal is worth arguing, better handled by amendment, or, sometimes, not worth the money at all. A response we do not believe in is a response we will tell you not to buy.

Applications we did not file

Takeover matters are welcome. If your application was filed by another attorney, by a filing service, or on your own, we can appear as counsel and respond.

The fee

Flat, per response, quoted in writing after we review the Office Action, before any work begins.
