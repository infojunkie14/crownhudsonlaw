Madrid Protocol §66(a) provisional refusals.

Your international registration reached the United States. The USPTO said no. This is the firm that answers.

What happened to your U.S. designation

When an international registration under the Madrid Protocol designates the United States, the USPTO examines that request for extension of protection like any U.S. application. If the examining attorney finds a problem, the USPTO issues a provisional refusal, transmitted to you through WIPO. The refusal looks alarming and arrives with unfamiliar U.S. citations, but it is an ordinary, answerable step in U.S. examination.

Why you need U.S. counsel now

A foreign-domiciled holder cannot respond through its home-country representative. Responding to a USPTO refusal requires an attorney licensed in the United States (37 C.F.R. § 2.11), appearing as your counsel of record. That is precisely the role this firm takes: we review the refusal, advise on the realistic paths, prepare and file the response, and remain your U.S. counsel through the life of the matter.

How we work with your existing advisors

Many holders reach us through the IP firm that manages their international portfolio. We are built for that arrangement: your home-country firm keeps the client relationship and the global strategy, and we carry the U.S. file, reporting in clean, forwardable English at every step. Holders who come to us directly are equally welcome.

What a response can involve

  • Argument against substantive refusals, including likelihood of confusion and descriptiveness.
  • Amendments to the identification of goods and services to satisfy USPTO practice, which is stricter than WIPO's classification standards.
  • Disclaimers, translations, and the procedural fixes U.S. examination requires.
  • Plain advice when narrowing, or letting a weak designation go, serves the brand better than a fight.

The fee

Flat, per response, quoted in writing after we review the refusal. Deadlines on Madrid refusals are unforgiving, so send the document early.
