U.S. trademark registration for foreign applicants.

From clearance to certificate, handled by U.S.-licensed counsel, on one flat fee quoted in writing.

Why foreign applicants need U.S. counsel

Since August 2019, U.S. law has required every trademark applicant, registrant, or party domiciled outside the United States to be represented before the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office by an attorney licensed in the United States (37 C.F.R. § 2.11). A non-lawyer filing service cannot lawfully represent you, and applications filed without required counsel run into trouble quickly. If your business lives abroad and your brand needs protection in the American market, a U.S. attorney is not optional. This firm exists to be that attorney.

What the engagement covers

  • Clearance search and a candid risk read. Before anything is filed, we search existing federal registrations and applications and tell you plainly what stands in the way. If the mark has a serious problem, you hear it before you spend, not after.
  • Goods and services identification. We draft your identification using USPTO-accepted language and select the correct international classes, decisions that shape both cost and scope of protection.
  • Filing basis and strategy. Use in commerce, intent to use, or a foreign registration basis: we choose the route that fits how your business actually operates.
  • Preparation, filing, and prosecution. We draft and file the application, monitor it, respond to examination issues as they arise, and carry the matter through to registration.
  • Plain-language reporting. Every milestone is reported by email, in language written for business owners, not for other lawyers.

Who this is for

Brand owners domiciled outside the United States, including e-commerce and marketplace sellers whose platforms expect a U.S. registration; international companies entering the American market; and the clients of foreign IP firms that need U.S. counsel of record. U.S.-domiciled applicants are welcome as well.

The fee

One flat fee, quoted in a written engagement before any work begins. USPTO government fees are separate and passed through at cost. No hourly billing, ever.
