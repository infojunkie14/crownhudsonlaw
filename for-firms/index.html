For International IP Firms

Your client. Your relationship. Our signature on the U.S. file.

U.S. counsel of record for the firms that manage international portfolios, on flat fees quoted in writing.

The arrangement

Your foreign-domiciled clients must be represented before the USPTO by a U.S.-licensed attorney (37 C.F.R. § 2.11), and you need one who leaves the client relationship where it belongs. Crown Hudson Law appears as counsel of record, does the U.S. work, and reports to you. The client relationship and the global strategy remain yours, and we work through your firm. We never market to a firm's client, and every matter begins with a conflict check.

What firms send us

  • New U.S. applications for foreign-domiciled clients, including clearance and identification drafting.
  • Office Action responses, whether or not we filed the underlying application.
  • Madrid Protocol § 66(a) provisional refusals on U.S. designations.
  • Counsel-of-record appearances where U.S. representation is required and the file is otherwise yours.

Built for cross-border work

The practice is fully online, which suits a firm nine time zones away: instructions and reports move in writing, on your schedule, and every step of the file is documented. Reports are written in clean English you can forward to your client unedited, and turnaround expectations are stated in the engagement.

Flat fees, quoted in writing

Fees are flat and quoted in writing per matter type, so your firm knows the U.S. cost before it instructs us. The fee we quote is the fee we charge; USPTO filing fees pass through at cost, itemized in the engagement.

Start with one file

The sensible first step is a single matter: one refusal, one filing. See how the reporting reads and how the deadlines are kept, then decide what to send next.
